Merchant admin information architecture

The StackBack admin grew to 14 flat nav items in the order they were built, with no grouping logic, and Shopify truncates the list. This refactor takes it to 11 while adding two modules that were never in the nav at all. It changes navigation and naming only: no permissions layer, nothing hidden from anyone, no auth work.

02

Why this order

The sidebar is Shopify's App Bridge nav: a flat list, with no nesting, no section headers and no group labels. Order is the only structural signal available, so it has to be derivable rather than memorised.

PRINCIPLE 1

Data lineage, parent before child

The nav mirrors the object graph. You cannot have a subscription without a plan, or an order without a subscription. A six-delivery prepaid contract is one row in Subscriptions and six rows in Orders, so the parent sits above its children.

PRINCIPLE 2

Derived views sit under their source

Payment Reminders is Orders filtered to unpaid. Inventory is Orders summed by SKU and date, looking forward. Neither exists without Orders, so both follow it rather than competing with it for position.

PRINCIPLE 3

Configuration sinks into Settings

Anything set once and revisited rarely goes inside Settings rather than beside daily work: branding, integrations, billing, policies, and the customer notification templates.

Why not order by how often a screen is opened. That was the first instinct and it is wrong here. Frequency is not a property a merchant can see, so the sequence carries no visible logic and has to be memorised, and every new module inserted by frequency reshuffles it.

A lineage order is derivable. Understand that a plan produces a subscription which produces orders, and you can predict where anything new will land. When Try Before You Buy ships it goes inside Plans, because that is what it is, and nothing moves.

The fold, and what version A accepts about it. App Bridge truncates the list behind a "View more" control. Today that cut falls at an arbitrary point, so the hidden items are effectively random.

At 11 items version A still gets cut, and Analytics and Settings fall below the fold. That is an accepted cost of the locked choice, and the least bad place for the cut to land: both are periodic rather than daily, and both belong to the in-and-out personas. Versions B and C avoided the fold entirely, and that advantage was weighed and not taken.

04

Three versions were designed

Same lineage logic and the same naming in all three. They differ only in how much they collapse. B and C are kept as documented alternatives, because their arguments stay relevant if the item count becomes a problem later.

Version C quietly undoes two decisions already made. Orders carrying Forecast and Unpaid tabs is structurally the unified "Orders and Inventory" hub that was superseded after six wireframe iterations, just renamed. It also dissolves Payment Reminders, which was kept deliberately, twice.

C is the tidiest list and that is a real advantage, but it wins on count by reversing considered decisions. It should be taken deliberately, not by accident.

05

Open calls before build

Five things are unresolved. Two need an answer before dev starts; the rest can be revisited once the refactor is live.

The bell, or banners instead?
An embedded app cannot add anything to Shopify's own top bar, so a persistent alert bell needs StackBack's own header row inside the content frame. That is permanent chrome costing vertical space on all 11 screens. The cheaper alternative is no bell: alerts surface as a card on Home, and anything urgent banners the screen it concerns, so a failed push banners Orders. Less discoverable, zero chrome cost.
Needs App Bridge confirmation · coordinate with INT-01, already in review
Which brand-settings source is authoritative?
Brand fields exist in both Settings and Portal Settings today. That duplication is real and predates this refactor. A data audit has to establish which one is live before they consolidate into Settings and Branding.
Blocks the Branding consolidation
Does the fold hurt Analytics?
Analytics is brand new and sits below "View more" in version A. If founders cannot find it, the fix is reordering inside the locked structure, not a new version.
Revisit after launch
Does Checkout compatibility survive two levels deep?
That status is what Operations needs under pressure during a live checkout incident, and it now sits inside Settings and Integrations. Mitigations are a setup-health warning on Home and making Settings search match "checkout".
Revisit after the first real incident
Personas are still incomplete
The store list is current at seven pilot stores, but what an Operations person actually does in a week is inferred rather than observed. Closing this would validate or correct Inventory, the least evidenced screen in the spec.
Research task, not a blocker
